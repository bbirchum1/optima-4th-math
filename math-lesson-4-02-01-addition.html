Today's Mission
Take the "add by place" idea from last unit and turn it into a full method. Stack two multi-digit numbers by place, add one column at a time starting from the ones, and carry whenever a column hits ten.
❓
Essential Question
Why does adding place by place — starting from the ones — actually work?

You added by place once in Unit 1 and the answer matched your estimate. Today you'll learn the same move as a full algorithm — the way a mathematician writes it down.

The trick: stack the digits by place. Add each column. When a column sum reaches 10 or more, carry the extra to the next place. That's it. The carry is just place value's quiet rule: ten of any place equals one of the next place over.

📐 What You Just Saw

The standard algorithm for addition.

Step 1 — Stack the addends by place. Ones under ones. Tens under tens. Always line them up.

Step 2 — Add each column, starting from the ones and moving left. Write the sum below.

Step 3 — Carry when a column sum is 10 or more. The ones digit of the sum goes in that place. The tens digit (the 1) gets added into the next column to the left.

That's the whole procedure. The carry is just place value's quiet rule — ten ones make one ten, ten tens make one hundred, and so on. The algorithm respects that rule automatically.

🔗 The Algorithm, Column by Column

Here's every column of 34,587 + 18,259. Each row shows the column sum, what gets written, and whether a carry happens.

Column Sum Write / Carry
Ones 7 + 9 = 16 Write 6 · carry 1 → tens
Tens 1 + 8 + 5 = 14 Write 4 · carry 1 → hundreds
Hundreds 1 + 5 + 2 = 8 Write 8 · no carry
Thousands 4 + 8 = 12 Write 2 · carry 1 → ten-thousands
Ten-thousands 1 + 3 + 1 = 5 Write 5 · no carry. Sum: 52,846

The procedure never changes. Every column: add what's there, add any carry from the right, write one digit, send any extra digit to the next column. The carries are place value's way of saying "this place is full — move up."

Algorithm vs Partial Sums
Two ways to add 24,683 + 17,259. Same answer, different journey.

Two students added the same two numbers. One used the standard algorithm. The other broke each number apart by place first, then added the parts.

STUDENT A — Standard Algorithm
"Stack and add column by column from ones.
Ones 3+9=12 (write 2, carry 1).
Tens 1+8+5=14 (write 4, carry 1).
Hundreds 1+6+2=9.
Thousands 4+7=11 (write 1, carry 1).
Ten-thousands 1+2+1=4.
Answer: 41,942."
STUDENT B — Partial Sums
"Break each by place.
20,000 + 10,000 = 30,000
4,000 + 7,000 = 11,000
600 + 200 = 800
80 + 50 = 130
3 + 9 = 12
Add them all: 30,000 + 11,000 + 800 + 130 + 12 = 41,942."
🧐

Same answer. Student A's algorithm is faster once you trust it. Student B's partial sums show WHY the algorithm works — every column sum is just the partial sum at that place. The algorithm is partial sums, organized neatly.

Preview Lesson 4.02.02 — Subtraction

Today you learned to ADD multi-digit numbers by place — column by column, with carries when a column reaches 10. The chart never went away; the algorithm just organized place value into a routine.

Next lesson, we'll do the same thing in REVERSE. Subtraction undoes addition. You'll line numbers up the same way, but now when a column doesn't have enough to subtract from, you'll regroup from the next place — borrow 1 hundred and turn it into 10 tens, for example. Same chart, same place value, new direction.
